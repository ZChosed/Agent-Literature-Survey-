## Design decisions worth knowing

**Schema before search.** The schema is derived top-down from proposal text,
not bottom-up from what papers happen to report. §2.2 declines to write "yet
another survey of agent-based approaches to manufacturing [6,23,24,27]" and
asks instead for "features that are common with our definition, as well as
those that differ" — a structured comparison against [7,8,10], not a narrative.

**Evidence quotes are mandatory.** Every substantive field returns
`{value, evidence_quote, page, confidence}`, max 25 verbatim words. This is the
primary hallucination control and it answers §3.3 directly: foundation models
"require validation through deterministic tools or human oversight" [26].

**No LLM ever counts anything.** Retrieval, deduplication, scoring and all
aggregation happen in Python. §3.3 records that foundation models "exhibit
inconsistencies in numerical accuracy"; §2.4 repeats it. Designing a pipeline
that ignores the project's own background section would be indefensible in the
report.

**`explores_beyond_predefined_env` is the load-bearing field.** §2.2 and §3.2
both frame the research gap in exactly those terms — prior agents "were not
able to explore beyond their pre-defined environment." Expect a strong majority
of `no`. That distribution is what justifies the Phase II case study.

**Expansion direction is per seed, not per run.** (v1.2.0) A single `expand`
flag could not express what the seed set needs, and the cost was concentrated
in one role. Summing forward edges from the live run's `cited_by_count` column:

| Role | Seeds | Forward edges | Share |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `dt_survey` | 6 | 3,691 | 74.2% |
| `dt_core` | 3 | 504 | 10.1% |
| `agent_related` | 7 | 349 | 7.0% |
| `framework_core` | 3 | 150 | 3.0% |
| others | 16 | 283 | 5.7% |

A 25:1 ratio against the signal. [14] Liu (1,877 citations) and [19] Mihai
(1,034) alone were 58% of the entire forward pull, and what cites a generic
digital-twin survey is the digital-twin field — which §2.1 explicitly declines
to re-survey. Left alone, screening meant reading several hundred DT papers to
find a handful of agent papers, and the 600-cap was silently discarding 1,711
of those edges anyway.

But those surveys' *reference lists* are genuinely wanted: they are the source
for the ~15-paper DT comparison subset. One flag cannot say "cite-list yes,
citations no", so there are now two: `expand_backward` and `expand_forward`.

**All three of the rules this motivated have since been withdrawn.** `standards`
→ forward only and [35] Zhang → forward only went in v1.3.0; `dt_survey` →
backward only went in v1.4.0, when it was finally measured and the measurement
did not support it. The counting above is still correct — dt_survey really did
carry 74.2% of forward edges — but *volume is not topicality*, and the second
step was never more than a plausible story about what those citing works
contain. See "Seed precision is measured, not asserted" below.

What survives from v1.2.0 is the mechanism, not the rules: `expand_backward` and
`expand_forward` are still separate columns, and a pre-v1.2.0 `seeds.csv`
carrying only the single `expand` column still loads with its value applied to
both. Today those columns encode **data availability only** — the four seeds
OpenAlex has no record of are off in both directions, and all 31 resolvable
seeds are on in both. Every topical decision has moved into the measured
policy.

Where that leaves the edge budget, at the current 600/600 caps:

| | Backward | Forward | Total |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Single `expand` flag (v1.1.0) | 2,555 | 3,266 | 5,821 |
| Both v1.2.0 rules | 2,538 | 1,287 | 3,825 |
| v1.3.0, asserted rules withdrawn | 3,135 | 1,287 | **4,422** |

Withdrawing the two unmeasured gates costs 597 backward edges' worth of extra
screening — and buys back the ability to say *why* any of those papers ranked
where they did. The forward saving, which is the one that was actually
measured, is untouched: agent-side forward share stays at 48.6%. Those 3,135
references dedupe to roughly 2,400 unique works, about 48 batched requests.

**Seed precision is measured, not asserted.** (v1.3.0) An earlier version of
this pipeline disabled backward expansion for [35] Zhang on the stated ground
that its 463 references were "nearly all general machine learning, not
manufacturing," and disabled it for the whole `standards` role on a similar
reading of what those bibliographies contain. Neither was a criterion. Both
were recall — not measurable, not reproducible, and in [35]'s case an n-of-1
rule written as though it were policy. Both have been removed; backward
expansion is on again for those seeds.

What replaces them is a quantity computed from metadata already in flight.

*The target domain.* §2.2 defines this whole review as a comparison against the
framework in [7, 8, 10]. So the target is that framework's own topical
fingerprint rather than a hand-written topic list:

> **T** = union of OpenAlex `topics` at score ≥ 0.3 carried by seeds [7], [8],
> [9] and [10].

([9] is the same architecture line by the same group. The seed list and the
score floor are set in `expansion_policy.json`, not in the code.) Note this is
`topics`, not the deprecated `concepts`.

*The measurement.* For each seed *s*:

> **p(s)** = fraction of *s*'s references carrying at least one topic in **T**

Deterministic, reproducible, no LLM judgment, and free: the reference works are
fetched for expansion anyway, so p(s) costs no extra requests.

*The use.* p(s) is a **weight on scoring, not a gate on retrieval**. The
ranking term is now

> **score(c)** = Σ p(s) over every seed *s* linking to candidate *c*

replacing the raw `n_seed_links` integer, which stays in `candidates.csv` as the
unweighted audit trail. A paper reached only from a low-precision survey scores
near zero; one reached from [10] scores around p([10]); one reached from both
scores their sum and correctly outranks either. Screening then works down the
ranked list under a stopping rule, and **the stopping point — not a retrieval
gate — is the thing to report and defend.**

Two reasons the weight is the right instrument here:

- **Cost doesn't justify a gate.** Reference lists arrive attached to the seed
  record, already paid for. The only marginal cost is batch-fetching the
  referenced works at 50/request: roughly 3,100 references dedupe to about
  2,400 works, ~48 requests, a few seconds. No retrieval budget is being
  protected. The only real cost is screening volume.
- **Exclusion is unauditable; downweighting isn't.** A paper never retrieved
  leaves no trace in the record, so a reviewer asking "why isn't Smith 2023 in
  here?" gets no answer. A paper retrieved, ranked low and screened out has an
  explicit reason attached. For a methods section that has to survive NIST
  review, that asymmetry matters more than queue length.

**The optional hard gate, and when it is legitimate.** There is one good reason
to want a gate: a flooded screening queue inflates Cohen's κ on easy negatives
and makes the agreement statistic less meaningful. If that becomes the concern,
`--backward-gate` enables the pre-registered rule in `expansion_policy.json`:

> Disable backward expansion for seed *s* **iff**
> U(*s*) ≥ 150 **and** p_unique(*s*) < 0.5 · p̄_core

where U(*s*) is the references reached from *s* and no other seed, and p̄_core is
the mean precision of the `framework_core` seeds. **Both conditions are
necessary.** A large reference list is fine if it's on-topic; a low-precision
one is fine if it's small, because it barely moves the queue. Only the
combination — large *and* dilute — justifies exclusion. Precision for the gate
is computed on U(*s*) rather than all of R(*s*): shared references are
corroborated by another seed and cost nothing.

It is **off by default**, because the weight is the defensible instrument and a
gate removes papers from the record entirely.

Two guards on the estimate, both in the policy file:

- **Minimum sample.** p(s) on fewer than 30 references is noise — [11] Kusiak
  has 4 indexed references. Below the minimum, the weight falls back to the mean
  of same-role seeds that clear the bar, and `precision_basis` records the
  substitution. (The gate's own volume condition, U ≥ 150, already exceeds the
  floor, so gate decisions are never made on a small sample.)
- **Zero-reference seeds.** [16], [21] and [37] have empty OpenAlex reference
  lists. That is a data gap, not a precision finding, and is coded
  `no_references` so it cannot be read as an editorial judgment. If nothing at
  all is measurable, weighting goes uniform at 1.0 rather than zero — "could not
  measure" must not silently become "counts for nothing."

**What gets recorded.** `corpus/seed_precision.csv` and the `seed_precision`
block of `provenance.json` carry, per seed: p_all, p_unique, U(s), |R(s)|, the
resolved-reference count, the precision basis, the threshold in force, the
decision and which rule fired — plus **T** itself. Together with the committed
policy file, that turns "we excluded a seed" into "the rule excluded a seed,
here are its inputs, here is the commit that fixed the thresholds beforehand."

**The forward mirror: q(s).** (v1.4.0) Backward references ride free on the seed
record; citing works do not, which is why the forward direction stayed asserted
one version longer. It is now measured the same way:

> **q(s)** = fraction of works *citing* seed *s* that carry a topic in **T**

estimated from a **deterministic random sample** — OpenAlex `sample=200&seed=42`,
which returns the same works on every run. Two requests per seed: one count over
the min-year window for the denominator, one sample. This is the distinction the
truncation warnings elsewhere in this file are about: a pagination-order cap
takes whatever the API happens to return first and is therefore a hidden
inclusion criterion, while a seeded random sample is an unbiased estimator that
happens to be reproducible.

Uniqueness is **exact, not estimated**: a sampled citing work's own
`referenced_works` says which other seeds it cites, so "cites *s* and no other
seed" is decidable per sampled work. The unique fraction of the sample scales
the window total into `est_unique_citations`, the volume term the forward gate
tests.

Each edge is then weighted by its seed's precision *in the direction that edge
was followed* — p(s) backward, q(s) forward. A seed reaching the same candidate
both ways contributes once, at the higher of the two: summing would let one seed
outweigh two genuinely independent ones, which is the opposite of what seed
breadth measures.

**The forward gate is ON by default; the backward gate is OFF.** That asymmetry
is deliberate. Backward retrieval is already paid for by the seed record, so a
backward gate buys only queue length and the weight strictly dominates. Forward
retrieval is a paged query per seed of up to `--max-forward-per-seed` works, so a
forward gate saves real retrieval *and* queue volume — and a forward-gated seed
still leaves a trace (its sample, q(s), the threshold, the rule that fired),
which a never-retrieved backward reference does not. Disable with
`--no-forward-gate`.

### What the measurement actually said

Measured 2026-09-21 against live OpenAlex: 152 requests, 31 of 35 seeds
resolved, `sample=200`, `seed=42`, min-year 2015. **T** came out as five topics,
all in Industrial & Manufacturing Engineering: Scheduling and Optimization
Algorithms, Digital Transformation in Industry, Manufacturing Process and
Optimization, Flexible and Reconfigurable Manufacturing Systems, Advanced
Manufacturing and Logistics Optimization.

p̄_core = 0.731 → backward threshold 0.365. q̄_core = 0.694 → forward threshold
0.347.

| Ref | Role | U(s) | p_unique | bwd | est. U | q_unique | fwd |
|---|---|---:|---:|:--|---:|---:|:--|
| [13] Leitão | `agent_related` | 462 | 0.344 | **gates** | 85 | 0.541 | — |
| [35] Zhang | `foundation_models` | 426 | 0.209 | **gates** | 30 | 0.300 | — |
| [23] Piperigkos | `survey_baseline` | 257 | 0.121 | **gates** | 13 | 0.231 | — |
| [14] Liu | `dt_survey` | 65 | 0.646 | — | 1,700 | 0.610 | — |
| [19] Mihai | `dt_survey` | 120 | 0.533 | — | 860 | 0.420 | — |
| [34] Xu | `dt_survey` | 126 | 0.452 | — | 236 | 0.305 | — |

Three things fell out of this, two of them against my own expectations.

**The [35] intuition was right, and [13] was not.** [35] Zhang measures
p_unique = 0.209, the second-lowest in the seed set — the withdrawn rule reached
the right conclusion by the wrong route. But [13] Leitão, which I expected to
stay comfortably on, measures 0.344 against a threshold of 0.365 and would gate
by a 6% margin. Its 515-reference bibliography is broader than "the
agent-manufacturing canon" suggests. The backward gate is off by default, so
neither currently fires.

**The `dt_survey` forward rule does not survive measurement.** [14] Liu and [19]
Mihai are the only seeds clearing the volume bar of 400, and both clear the
precision bar comfortably (0.610 and 0.420 against 0.347). The forward gate
fires on **nobody**. Taking the measurement seriously means the dt_survey
forward edges come back, and the forward queue returns to roughly its v1.1.0
size. That is the cost of the rule winning.

**But read that as abstention, not as clearance** — because of the next
paragraph.

**Honest limitation — and it is the load-bearing one.** **T is not the axis it
was supposed to be.** Its five topics are broad manufacturing-engineering
categories of 95k–245k works apiece, and not one of them is agent-specific. The
pipeline now measures this itself (`domain_discrimination`, warn-only, never
gates), and the numbers are blunt:

| | framework_core | other seeds | ratio | vs `dt_survey` |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| p(s) backward | 0.731 | 0.630 | 1.16 | **1.05** |
| q(s) forward | 0.694 | 0.541 | 1.28 | **1.15** |

A ratio of 1.05 means **T** cannot tell the framework seeds from the digital-twin
surveys. So p(s) and q(s) work as a *dilution filter* — they correctly isolate
[35]'s general-ML references and [23]'s automotive ones, which is real and
useful — but they **cannot adjudicate the agent-vs-DT distinction**, which is
exactly what the withdrawn `dt_survey` rule was trying to make. The forward gate
did not vindicate dt_survey forward expansion; it was never sensitive enough to
rule on it. Anything below `min_discrimination_ratio` (1.3) should be read that
way.

Two honest caveats on the pre-registration, while we are here. The thresholds in
`expansion_policy.json` were fixed before any p(s) or q(s) existed, which is the
claim that matters — but `min_unique_citations: 400` was chosen with the
forward-volume column of `seeds_resolved.csv` already visible, so it is
pre-registered with respect to the *measurement* and not with respect to the
*volume data*. It has not been touched since the measurement, and it should not
be: changing a threshold after seeing which seeds it catches is the exact
failure the file exists to prevent.

If `topics` proves too coarse to be worth keeping — and the 1.05 ratio is an
argument that it is — the transparent fallback is a pre-registered keyword list
matched against title + abstract. Cruder, but every decision traces to a term
you can point at, and it can be made agent-specific in a way OpenAlex's topic
tree apparently cannot.

**Honest limitation (classifier noise).** OpenAlex topic assignment is itself a
classifier with its own error rate, so p(s) and q(s) inherit that noise. q(s)
carries sampling error on top, roughly 0.5/√n — about ±3.5 points at n = 200, and
materially worse for the seeds whose entire citing set is smaller than the
sample. The raw counts are in `seed_precision.csv` so a reader can judge, rather
than any threshold being presented as objective.

One tension is now resolved rather than merely flagged. It used to be that
gating on an agent-axis **T** risked cutting the `dt_survey` reference lists that
supply the ~15-paper DT comparison subset. The measurement makes that moot in
both directions: **T** is not agent-specific enough to gate dt_survey on, and no
dt_survey seed gates on either side. `exempt_roles` remains available for the
case where a sharper **T** revives the problem; it is empty by default and every
entry needs a written justification in the policy file.

**A cap that bites is a silent inclusion criterion, so it gets reported.** The
backward cap moved 250 → 600, which clears the longest bibliography in the seed
set ([13] Leitão, 515 references). At 250, three seeds — [13], [23] Piperigkos
(352) and [35] Zhang (463) — were losing 580 references between them, and
*which* 580 was decided by OpenAlex pagination order. That is not a defensible
inclusion criterion for a systematic review. Any truncation now prints a `***`
warning at the end of the run and lands in `provenance.json` under
`truncations`, with the seed, direction, and how many edges were dropped. At
the current defaults (600/600) nothing truncates, and the list is empty.

**Citation velocity, not raw count, sets reading order.** Raw counts
systematically demote recent work; §2 calls these "emerging, exciting, and
fast-moving areas." Raw `cited_by_count` is still in the CSV — velocity only
decides what gets read first. (This changed in v1.1.0 after the smoke test
surfaced a 2016 paper outranking a 2025 LLM-agent paper.)

**Shared join keys with the Phase I schema.** `validation_level`,
`metrics_reported` and `application_domain` use identical enums across both
schemas, enforced by `validate_schemas.py`. §2.3 Phase III compares DT and
agent features on common axes; if the two reviews use different dimensions,
that comparison is unrecoverable without re-extraction. **Agree this spine with
the Phase I student before either of you extracts anything.**

## Scope change, 2026-10-05: accessible papers only

Decided at the 10/05 advisor meeting: **candidates are restricted to papers the
project can read.** `eligibility.py` applies the rule to every row of
`corpus/candidates.csv` and writes the decisions to **`corpus/eligibility.csv`**
— `openalex_id`, `eligible`, `exclusion_reason`, `eligibility_rule`,
`candidates_key_sha256`, one row per candidate in rank order. **It does not
modify `candidates.csv` and deletes nothing.** One rule:

- no OA copy — OpenAlex `open_access.is_oa` false **and** no Unpaywall OA
  location for the DOI (no DOI: OpenAlex alone) → `no_oa`
- otherwise → eligible, `none`

Publisher plays no part. Eligibility v1 also had a rule 1, DOI prefix `10.1016`
→ `elsevier` even when OA. It was **removed on 2026-10-05** (eligibility v2.0.0);
see "Rule 1 removed" below.

`is_oa` is fetched fresh, not inferred: `candidates.csv` only carried
`oa_pdf_url`, and an empty PDF link is not the same claim as `is_oa=false`. Every
OpenAlex and Unpaywall response is in `corpus/eligibility_calls.jsonl`, and
`python eligibility.py --offline` reproduces the decision from it with no
network. If any row cannot be decided, nothing is written.

### Rebuild-safe, and loud when stale

Eligibility lives in its own file because `corpus_build.py` rewrites
`candidates.csv` wholesale: decisions stored as columns there vanish on the
next build. Every consumer — `screen.py`, `draw_calibration_sample.py`,
`fulltext_head.py` — joins `eligibility.csv` on id through one loader,
`eligibility.load_eligibility()`, which **exits** if the file is missing or
stale. It never falls back and never defaults a row to eligible.

"Stale" means its `candidates_key_sha256` differs from the current
`candidates.csv`. **The key is the SHA-256 of the ordered (openalex_id, doi)
pairs, not of the whole file**, and that is deliberate. `fulltext_head.py` has
to run *after* eligibility, because it only targets eligible rows, and it
rewrites `candidates.csv` to add its own column. A whole-file hash would
therefore go stale on every normal run, while telling you nothing about
eligibility. The key covers exactly what the decision and the rank strata
depend on, so a rebuild that adds, drops, reorders or re-DOIs any row is
caught. Tests cover all four cases, and also confirm that a `fulltext_head`
rewrite does **not** trip it. The whole-file hash at decision time is still
recorded in provenance, for information.

After any rebuild: `python eligibility.py --offline` if the call log covers
the new rows, otherwise with `--mailto`.

### Two exclusion stages

"Excluded at eligibility" is a scope decision Python made before any model ran.
"Excluded at screening" is a model's `exclude` on an eligible paper. They are
never summed. `screen.py` sends ineligible rows **nowhere** — no model call, no
token count, no forced `uncertain`, no `calls.jsonl` row — and lists them under
`excluded_at_eligibility` in the run manifest. They are out of scope, not
uncertain. `benchmark.py` drops any labelled paper in that list from the
scoring denominator and reports the two stages on separate lines and in
separate columns (`n_excluded_at_eligibility`, `n_excluded_at_screening`).

## Stage 2 — screening calibration and model benchmark

Stage 1 produces a ranked corpus. Stage 2 answers a different question: **can a
model be trusted to screen it, and which one.** That answer is itself a
deliverable — proposal §2.2 asks for a report on how well the AI tools
performed — so the benchmark is built as instrumentation, and every model call
is logged and priced.

The governing rule does not change (§3.3): **LLMs classify; Python counts.**
`screen.py` obtains decisions; `benchmark.py` computes every number from files
on disk. No κ, rate, count or cost in this pipeline is produced by a model.

### Retrieval policy: the seed columns, and nothing else (v1.6.0)

Both hard gates and the `min_discrimination_ratio` diagnostic have been
**removed** — from the script, the policy file, the provenance record and the
tests. `expansion_policy.json` is now v2.0.0 and governs **ranking only**.

> The only retrieval policy is the `expand_backward` / `expand_forward`
> columns of `seeds.csv`. Nothing computed at run time decides what is
> retrieved.

They earned removal on their own measurements. The forward gate fired on
**zero seeds** at v1.5.0, because the only seeds with enough forward volume to
trip it were the six `dt_survey` seeds that `seeds.csv` already turns off by
hand. And the discrimination ratio the gates leaned on measured 1.05–1.3
against its own 1.3 floor — **T** barely separates `framework_core` from
`dt_survey` — so a gate that failed to fire was *abstaining*, not clearing. A
rule that cannot act, and could not justify acting if it did, is not a policy;
it is machinery that makes the record harder to read.

p(s) and q(s) survive as **ranking weights**. They still say which seed a
candidate came from, which is a real signal for ordering a screening queue, and
a low-weighted paper is still in `candidates.csv` with its weight attached.
What they no longer do is remove anything.

**Verified after the change:** 1,941 candidates, byte-identical set *and*
identical rank order to v1.5.0, and `screening/calibration_sample.csv`
unchanged to the checksum. Removing the gates changed nothing about what was
retrieved, which is exactly what "they never bit" predicts.

are gone. Eligibility requires an OA copy, so an eligible `10.1016` paper goes

**eligible** rows lacking an abstract, and leaves ineligible rows untouched.

of those rows had cached `no_elsevier_key` outcomes from the v1 Elsevier path.
That path overwrote the OA attempt's own outcome, so those entries are no

and 56 eligible rows (4.9% of eligible) have no text.** Three transient

`--refresh-errors` retry and are still open. The table below is the 09-28 run,
kept for the record.

431 rows (22.2%) still had no abstract after the Semantic Scholar backfill.
`fulltext_head.py` recovers the **first 5,000 characters** of the full text —
a character count, not a token estimate, with no model involved — from OA PDFs

cache under `corpus/fulltext/`, which is gitignored and never committed.

The head goes in its own `fulltext_head` column. **The `abstract` column is not

| Outcome | n | |
|---|---:|---|
| recovered (`fulltext_head_oa`) | **20** | |

### Two calibration sets, never pooled

A truncated full-text head opens with title-page matter and an introduction, is
cut off mid-sentence, and says nothing about results. That is a different input
distribution from an abstract, so it gets its own calibration set and its own
metrics, at the same floors (κ ≥ 0.7, include recall ≥ 0.95). One κ over both
would describe neither, and would hide the case that matters most — a model
reliable on abstracts and unreliable on heads.

The screening prompt is **identical except for the labelled body**. The head is
labelled as a head, and the label says explicitly that it is not an abstract
and that a missing result may simply be past the truncation. The blind sheet's
text column is named `fulltext_head` for the same reason: a rater who thinks
they are reading an abstract will read an absent result as a result the paper
does not report.

**Two things to know about the 20-paper set before trusting a κ from it.**

1. **It is still a census, not a sample.** The pool was exactly 20 on
   2026-09-28, so the draw took all of it and the RNG seed selected nothing.
   Metrics describe these 20 exactly and generalise no further. It is
   **deliberately left as drawn** after the 10-05 scope change — not redrawn,
   not archived. The Elsevier key that would have grown the pool is gone with
   the Elsevier path, so the census will not become a sample that way. Since
   rule 1 was removed, **all 20 are eligible**, including the 5 `10.1016` OA
   copies that v1 had excluded, so `screen.py` screens all 20. The eligible
   head pool today is 21: the 20, plus one head recovered on 10-05.
2. **Two of the 20 are not papers** — a journal masthead (`IEEE Transactions on
   Instrumentation and Measurement`) and a preprint-server advertisement
   (`TechRxiv: Share Your Preprint Research With the World!`), both typed
   `article` by OpenAlex. They are legitimate `exclude` decisions and are left
   in rather than silently filtered, but they are *easy* negatives, and easy
   negatives inflate κ. On a set of 20 that is 10% of the denominator.

**Eligible** rows with neither an abstract nor a head (27 as of 10-05) are
**forced to `uncertain` in Python with no model call**. Ineligible rows are
never forced: they are out of scope, not uncertain. `uncertain` routes to a human, which is already
the only correct outcome when there is no evidence to apply the criteria to;
paying a model to read a bare title and return a predetermined answer would
buy nothing and would put a fabricated judgement in the record. Those rows are
logged with `validation_status: "forced"` and `cost_usd: 0.0`, and
`benchmark.py` counts them separately — agreement on a forced row is agreement
with a rule, not with a model.

### The abstract gap, and what measuring it actually showed

OpenAlex removed Elsevier-sourced abstracts in 2022. **629 of 1,941 candidates
(32.4%) came back with no abstract** — inside the 20–40% band expected from
that removal. Semantic Scholar backfill by DOI closed it to **431 (22.2%)**:
198 filled, 368 papers S2 knows but holds no abstract for, 62 DOIs S2 has no
record of, 1 row with no DOI, and zero unrecovered transient failures.

The gap tracks a publisher, the publisher tracks certain venues, and venue
plausibly correlates with rank — so I expected the missing abstracts to cluster
in part of the ranked list, and cut the strata *after* the abstract filter on
that basis. **Measured, that did not hold.** The full-list top third is 22.7%
missing and the bottom two-thirds 21.9% — flat. So the filter-then-cut ordering
turned out to be immaterial rather than important, and the reason to keep it is
now that it is the ordering that stays correct if a future corpus *does* skew,
not that this one did. Both rates are in the archived v1 block,
`screening/archive/v1/calibration_sample_provenance.json`, under
`abstract_gap_by_full_list_third`, so the claim stays checkable rather than
becoming folklore.

Screening input is title + abstract only. A row with no abstract is therefore
not a row that screens badly; it is a row that **cannot be screened on the
stated inputs at all.** Two consequences, handled differently:

- **Calibration.** Rows without abstract text are excluded from the draw. A κ
  computed partly over title-only decisions measures two tasks at once. Since
  eligibility v2.0.0 (rule 1 removed) the pool is also restricted to eligible
  rows: **1,070** of 1,941 (794 ineligible, 77 eligible without an abstract),
  cut into thirds of 356 and 714. The current (v3) draw, same method and seed
  20260928, shares 2 papers with v1 and 3 with v2. Today 18 of the v1 50 are
  ineligible and none of the v2 50 are. Both earlier draws are archived in
  `screening/archive/v1/` and `screening/archive/v2/`. Pool exclusion by
  full-list third is 42.8% top vs 45.9% bottom
  (`calibration_sample.pool_exclusion_by_full_list_third`).
- **Production.** They stay in the corpus, screen on title, and are forced to
  `uncertain` — which routes them to a human rather than dropping them.

`abstract_backfill.py` distinguishes *why* each row is still empty — S2 has no
record of the DOI, S2 has the paper but no abstract, or the request failed —
because only the last kind is fixable by rerunning. `unrecovered_transient_failures`
in provenance says whether the final `none` count is a finding or an upper bound.

### The draw is stratified, and that changes what κ means

25 from the top third of the eligible ranked list, 25 from the bottom
two-thirds, fixed RNG seed, shuffled before writing. A uniform 50 from 1,941
would hold two or three includes, and both κ and include-recall would be
estimated from that denominator.

The price is that **κ on this sample is not an estimate of κ over the corpus.**
The head is deliberately oversampled 2:1. `stratum` is carried on every sample
row so a later analysis can reweight, and provenance says so in as many words
rather than leaving it to be inferred.

Thirds are cut on the *eligible* list, after the abstract filter. Since that
choice rests on the assumption that the gap is rank-correlated, the script
measures the assumption: per-third missing-abstract rates go into provenance, so
a reader can see whether the ordering mattered at all.

`labels_TODO.csv` carries paper_id, title and abstract and nothing else. Stratum,
rank, seed links, score and the framework_core flag are all signals about how the
pipeline already ranked a paper; a rater who can see them is not an independent
rater, and the κ would measure agreement with the ranking rather than agreement
about the criteria.

### What the screening harness measures about itself

- **Validation failures are logged and never retried.** A silent retry converts
  a measured malformed-output rate into an unmeasurable one. `strict: true` is
  off by default for the same reason: schema enforcement at the API would make
  the client-side validator dead code, and "how often did the model emit
  something unusable" is a Report 2 finding.
- **A hallucinated `criterion_id` is a validation failure** — but only when the
  ID set parsed out of `criteria.md` is trustworthy (≥2 IDs found). Below that,
  membership is recorded and not enforced, because a parser that found nothing
  is evidence about the parser, not about the model. The mode is printed before
  any money is spent and recorded in the manifest.
- **Isolation is checked, not just intended.** `calibration_sample.csv` carries
  a stratum column, so `_isolation_check` asserts at render time that no
  forbidden column's value reached the prompt.
- **Both hashes are logged.** `criteria.md`'s SHA-256 alone cannot detect a
  change in the instructions wrapped *around* the criteria, so the full system
  prompt is hashed too.
- **Cost is estimated before submission and recomputed from actual usage
  afterwards.** `benchmark.py` reports only the latter. Token counts come from
  `messages.count_tokens`, not from character-length guesswork; the one
  unavoidable assumption — output tokens per call — is named in the manifest.

### Three API facts that constrain the method

All three verified against `platform.claude.com` on 2026-09-28. Two of them
force a documented deviation from the brief.

**1. The brief's model IDs are correct and are used verbatim.** `claude-opus-5-5`,
`claude-sonnet-5-5` and `claude-haiku-4-5-20251001` are all current; the dated
Haiku form is its pinned snapshot, which is what reproducibility wants.
**`screen.py` never substitutes a model ID.** Every requested ID is checked
against `GET /v1/models` at startup and the run aborts, having submitted
nothing, if one is not served. A substitution would change which models the
benchmark is about, change the cost basis the selection rule picks on, and
leave a logged but unrequested change to the method.

Prices are pinned in `PRICING` with the source and date, and an unpriced model
is a hard abort rather than a $0.00 estimate — the selection rule picks the
cheapest, so a missing price would win on a fiction. Note the cache-read
multiplier is **per model**: 0.05× on Opus 5.5 ($0.20/MTok) against 0.10×
elsewhere.

| | Haiku 4.5 | Sonnet 5.5 | Opus 5.5 |
|---|---|---|---|
| in / out $ per MTok | 1 / 5 | 2 / 10 | 4 / 20 |
| cache read | 0.10× | 0.10× | **0.05×** |
| min cacheable prefix | **4,096** | 512 | 512 |
| forced `tool_choice` | supported | **400** | **400** |
| thinking off | omit field | `between_tools` | **impossible** |

**2. Forced tool use no longer exists on the 5.5 models,** so the structured
output is not a forced call. `tool_choice` `{"type":"any"}` and
`{"type":"tool",...}` return

```
400 tool_choice: type "tool" and "any" are not supported for this model.
```

on both Opus 5.5 and Sonnet 5.5, and the same check applies to the token
counting endpoint — so even the budget estimate would fail. The documented
replacement is `tool_choice: auto` plus `strict: true`, and that is what the
harness sends **to all three models**, including Haiku 4.5, which would still
accept forcing. A per-model request shape would make the benchmark compare
request shapes as well as models.

The cost is that a model may answer in prose instead of calling the tool. The
system prompt says the tool must be called for every paper, and a prose answer
is logged as a `no_tool_use` validation failure — a measurable rate, and a
finding about the model rather than a hidden failure.

**3. Thinking cannot be disabled on Opus 5.5,** so "no extended thinking" is
achievable on two models out of three:

- `claude-haiku-4-5-20251001` — omit the `thinking` field. No thinking, no
  effort parameter.
- `claude-sonnet-5-5` — `thinking: {"type":"between_tools"}`; `disabled`
  returns a 400 naming `between_tools`. Since screening makes one tool call and
  stops, there is no between-tool moment, so this is effectively no thinking.
- `claude-opus-5-5` — adaptive thinking is **always on**; both `disabled` and
  `enabled` return 400. `effort: "low"` is the only lever.

**This is a real confound in the cost comparison, not a footnote.** Opus 5.5's
thinking tokens are billed as output tokens, so it pays for something the other
two do not — and the selection rule picks the cheapest qualifying model.
`benchmark.py` carries `thinking_unavoidable` per model into `benchmark.csv`
and prints a warning with the table. The rule is still applied exactly as
specified; the comparison is just not like-for-like, and the record says so.

A consequence: `max_tokens` caps **total** output, thinking included. It is set
to 4,096 rather than a tight 300, because a tight cap would truncate every
Opus 5.5 call into a `max_tokens` validation failure and measure the cap rather
than the model. Output bills on tokens produced, so the headroom is close to
free.

### criteria.md, and what the harness had to change to read it

`screening/criteria.md` in the run order is `criteria.md` at the repo root as
written — pass `--criteria criteria.md`. Human written; the harness reads,
hashes and embeds it verbatim and does not author it.

Two things it broke, both now fixed and both pinned by tests:

**The ID parser saw none of its IDs.** The criteria define them as bare
line-start tokens — `I1 — Learning or exploratory agents.` — and the parser
only recognised markdown headings, bullets, table cells and bold text. With
zero IDs found, `--criterion-id-check auto` correctly declines to enforce
membership, so a hallucinated `criterion_id` would have passed validation in
silence. A fifth pattern now matches `ID` + dash at line start, which yields
exactly the 15 real IDs (I1–I4, D1, E1–E7, U1–U3) and still harvests nothing
from prose or from the decision procedure's mid-sentence references. A test
pins the parse against the real file, so a reformat that hides the IDs fails
loudly instead of quietly disabling the check.

**The system prompt carried a competing tie-break.** It used to say a false
exclude is unrecoverable and to prefer `uncertain` when torn against `exclude`.
That was written before criteria.md existed. The criteria now carry a complete
ordered decision procedure with their own tie-breaks, and theirs point the
other way in one place — *"Do not use uncertain because the paper seems only
moderately relevant."* Two sets of tie-break rules in one prompt would make the
uncertain rate a property of the prompt rather than of the criteria, and the
uncertain rate is a reported metric feeding the model comparison. The template
now states that the criteria are the only authority on the decision and
contains no decision rules of its own. It also no longer claims the input is
always an abstract.

**Cache minimums, now that the file has a real size.** The system prompt is
8,254 characters, roughly 2,060 tokens. That clears the 512-token minimum on
Opus 5.5 and Sonnet 5.5 — both will cache — and **falls short of Haiku 4.5's
4,096**, so the cached system block will not cache on the cheapest model. Not a
bug and not fixable by padding; just a fact to read the cost column with. The
logged `cache_creation_input_tokens` / `cache_read_input_tokens` settle it on
the first real run.

**Estimated batch cost**, from actual prompt sizes at current prices, all three
models:

| Run | n | mean input tok/call | est. cost |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| abstract sample | 50 | ~2,430 | **$0.78** |
| full-text sample | 20 | ~3,325 | **$0.37** |

Both well under the $2 target and the $10 `--max-usd` default. Opus 5.5 is
about 70% of each total, because its thinking cannot be disabled and those
tokens bill as output.

### The selection rule declines rather than compromises

Cheapest model with **binary κ ≥ 0.7 and include recall ≥ 0.95.** Binary κ is
primary because `uncertain` routes to a human, which is operationally the same
outcome as `include` — the paper survives. The 3-class κ is reported alongside
it because it answers a different question: whether the model uses `uncertain`
as the criteria intend or as a hedge.

Include recall carries the most weight because the two errors are not
symmetric. Screening is the only stage that removes papers on model output, and
**a false exclude is never seen again.** A false include costs one extraction.

If no model clears both floors, `benchmark.py` reports that and selects nothing.
The fix is to revise `criteria.md` using `disagreements.csv` and rerun — not to
lower a floor after seeing the results, which is the same error
`expansion_policy.json` exists to prevent on the retrieval side.
